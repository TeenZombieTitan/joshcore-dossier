Privacy

What's kept, and how to take it down.

This site publishes real information about real people who never filled in a form. That's the whole idea, and it's also exactly why this page exists. Here is everything, in plain terms.

Who runs this

Blackbox is a hobby project run by one person for one Discord server. There is no company behind it, nothing is sold, and there are no adverts, analytics, trackers or third-party scripts on this site.

What the bot records

While it's in the server, Blackbox keeps:

  • Your Discord ID, display name, username and avatar.
  • How many messages you send each day — including how many between 2am and 5am, which is where the "night owl" business comes from.
  • XP, level and reputation, and the titles and crowns you've been awarded.
  • Region, playstyle and platform — only if you set them yourself with ~setup.
  • Groups you hosted or joined, supply drops you claimed, rumours you filed.
  • Screenshots you post in the gallery channel, saved as files on the machine the bot runs on.
  • A log of things that happened: a crown awarded, a title earned, someone bounced from the server.

Message content

Your ordinary messages are counted, not stored. The bot increments a number and moves on — it does not keep a copy of what you said, and there is no way to read your chat history back out of it.

There are three exceptions, and they all involve text you deliberately put through the bot:

  • Read-aloud. If someone reacts 🔊 to a message, that message's text is stored in the event log so there's a record of what the bot was made to say out loud.
  • Rumours. A rumour you submit is stored and published — that's the point of submitting one.
  • Screenshot captions. Whatever you type alongside an image in the gallery channel is kept with the image.

Moderation reports also keep what was reported, so staff can act on them.

What ends up on this website

The site is rebuilt from that database every few minutes and published as plain files. It shows your name, avatar, level, XP, reputation, titles, crowns, the region and platform you set, a thirty-day activity chart, and any screenshots you posted.

Your Discord account ID is never published. Sign-in matches you to your page using a one-way hash of your ID, so the published files never contain a list of anyone's account numbers.

Signing in

Signing in is optional and does nothing except highlight your own entries. It uses Discord's own login with the identify permission — your ID, name and avatar. Not your email, not your servers, not your messages.

The token comes back to your browser, is used once to ask Discord who you are, and is then thrown away. Nothing about your sign-in is sent to a server, because for the public site there isn't one — it's static files. What's left is a note in your own browser's storage, which "sign out" deletes.

Taking yourself off

You do not have to ask anyone's permission for any of this.

  • ~hideme — seals your file. Your profile page stops existing, you come off the directory, and your screenshots come off the gallery. Run it again to undo it.
  • ~pull — reply to one of your screenshots in Discord to take that image off the site. Clicking the ❌ on your own post does the same thing.

One thing to be straight about: sealing your file does not remove your name from the standings. You still appear in the leaderboard with your name, level and XP — just with no link to a page. If you want off the standings as well, ask in the server and it'll be done by hand.

Deleting it properly

If you want your record actually deleted rather than hidden — gone from the database, not just off the site — ask in the Discord server and it will be removed. Leaving the server does not delete anything on its own.

How long it's kept

Indefinitely, unless you ask for it to go. This is a server history project; there is no automatic expiry, and that's deliberate.

Children

Discord requires its users to be at least 13, and older in some countries. This site inherits that — it isn't intended for anyone younger.

If this changes

The date at the bottom of every page is when the site was last rebuilt. If what's collected changes meaningfully, it'll be said in the server rather than quietly edited in here.

Written in plain English by the person who runs this, not by a lawyer. It describes honestly what the code actually does. If something here doesn't match what you see, that's a bug — say so in the server and it'll be fixed.
